Fly brains vs trading bots

We put real fruit-fly brains against trading bots on the same tokens, the same two weeks of real Robinhood Chain prices and the same costs. Every result is on this page, including the flies that lost.

What it shows

The flies lost less than every bot

The best bot lost 25%; five of the six flies lost less than that, the best only 4.3%. The bots lost mostly to churn: rotating the wallet every 12 hours costs 0.3–1% a swap on these thin pools. The flies trade smaller, pay about 0.13% impact a fill, and sat out more of a falling market.

Not every fly beat the market

Three of the six flies beat the market and three lost more than it. Each fly trades with its own settings, and this round shows which settings held up in a falling market. Every fly is listed above, and the next rounds will show whether the best ones keep winning.

The LLM bot came last

A language model shown each token's recent moves and asked what to hold, every 12 hours, lost 37%. It matches what we found before: no edge, and the churn costs the rest.

The rules

  • Prices: 15-minute candles of every token on fly.ai's list, from GeckoTerminal, used every 45 minutes (the live flies' pace).
  • Costs, on every trade, for everyone: the pool fee, price impact from each pool's real depth (constant product), and $0.05 gas.
  • Flies: the live fly brain (the full connectome, all learning on), six flies with their own settings.
  • Bots: buy-and-hold (every token, equal weight, bought once); momentum and reversal (every 12 h, hold the 3 tokens that rose or fell most over 24 h); random (3 random tokens every 12 h, averaged over 20 runs); the LLM bot (every 12 h, a language model picks up to 3 tokens from their 2 h, 6 h and 24 h moves). Bots only trade pools deep enough for their size.

One two-week window in a falling market. A ranking here is what happened on these prices, not proof of an edge. We will run it again on new windows and publish the result either way.
