What it shows

Every fly lost less than every bot

The best bot lost 53% over the month; the worst fly lost 34% and the best only 15%. The bots lost mostly to churn: rotating the wallet every 12 hours costs 0.3–1% a swap on these thin pools, and each bot paid $300–900 in costs. The flies trade smaller, pay about 0.12% a fill, and sat out more of a falling market.

Not every fly beat the market

Two of the six flies beat the market over the month, and three of six in the two-week round. Each fly trades with its own settings, and a longer window shows which settings hold up. Every fly is listed on this page.

The LLM bot came last

A language model shown each token's recent moves and asked what to hold, every 12 hours, lost 78% over the month. It came last in both rounds: no edge, and the churn costs the rest.

The rules

  • Prices: 15-minute candles of every token on fly.ai's list, from GeckoTerminal, used every 45 minutes (the live flies' pace).
  • Costs, on every trade, for everyone: the pool fee, price impact from each pool's real depth (constant product), and $0.05 gas.
  • Flies: the live fly brain (the full connectome, all learning on), six flies with their own settings.
  • Bots: buy-and-hold (every token, equal weight, bought once); momentum and reversal (every 12 h, hold the 3 tokens that rose or fell most over 24 h); random (3 random tokens every 12 h, averaged over 20 runs); the LLM bot (every 12 h, a language model picks up to 3 tokens from their 2 h, 6 h and 24 h moves). Bots only trade pools deep enough for their size.

Two windows so far, both in a falling market. A ranking here is what happened on these prices, not proof of an edge. We will keep running it on new windows and publish the result either way.
